How a mission works

Each mission is a section of about one-quarter square mile (a 1/2 mile by 1/2 mile square) with the affected buildings our model shows at the forecast crest. One team can usually finish one mission in a few hours. Tiny neighboring cells are combined; a cell with more than about 60 buildings is split so the work stays a reasonable size.

  1. Look at the map and the list. Filter by county, priority or status, or search for a town, street or mission ID.
  2. Open a mission to see the numbered houses, addresses, depths and the start point.
  3. Your team lead assigns the mission (status changes from Open to Assigned, then to Done when you finish). Please do not start a mission until your team lead has assigned it to you.
  4. Download or print the one-page mission PDF. It has the map, the address list and a checklist with space for notes.
  5. Check in before you go and check out when you are done, using the Volunteer Sign In / Out page, and with your team lead. If anyone is in danger, call 911.
  6. Send one field report per house you check, with the water depth, damage, a severity from 1 to 5 and photos. On the mission page, tap the house's address (in the list or on the map) to open its report form with the mission, Map ID and address already filled in. Photo upload needs a Google sign-in.

Do not enter floodwater. Eighteen inches or more of water means no vehicle access. These pages do not list staff names or phone numbers; your team lead will tell you how to check in.

Priority: High = 6 or more buildings with 18 in+ at crest, or any building 6 ft or deeper, or 3 or more buildings 3 ft or deeper. Medium = any 18 in+ at crest, or 10 or more buildings, or deepest class 18 in to 3 ft. Low = the rest. Grid cells are 0.5 mile on a side (about 0.25 square mile). Status is Open until a team lead assigns the mission. Rain-ponding buildings are included in the conservative crest set; they have ponding depth where the rain raster provides it, otherwise a 1 ft floor.
