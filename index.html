What it is

After the St. Johns River and heavy rain flooding of November 17-21, 2026, Stormy Weather Response Force (SWRF) made a flood map. It shows which homes and businesses the water likely reached, and about how deep the water got, now and at the NWS forecast crest.

Look up your address, or find your street on the house-level maps. Every building the river model reaches is drawn and numbered, with its address.

How it works

  1. Look up your address. Type an address, town or ZIP on the Flood lookup page. It only takes a minute.
  2. We check our flood map. We match the address to our map of where the water likely went and how deep it is at the building.
  3. Find your building on the detail maps. The house-level PDF maps (below) number every affected building; the Map ID on the lookup matches the map.
  4. We connect you with help. Use the Request help form; a volunteer reviews it.

How we estimate

  • River: we take live river levels from USGS gauges along the St. Johns, slope the water between them, and compare it with the ground height under every building. A building counts when the water reaches 1 ft or more at the building. We also report how many of those have 18 inches or more.
  • Rain: a screening estimate. We find low spots on the land, fill them with the rain that fell (radar estimate) minus what the drains are built to carry, and count buildings sitting in 1 ft or more of ponded water.
  • Reported areas: buildings inside neighborhoods where officials, NWS or the news reported flooding. Not all of them flooded.

Full methods, limits and sources

Good to know: these are estimates. They can be off in both directions. Added in Update 11/21 11:30 AM: Astor on a 3 m grid, Black Creek in Clay County, and the tide-affected St. Johns from Palatka to Jacksonville. Not modeled yet: smaller creeks.
