How we estimate

Updated Fri Nov 20, 2026, 8:45 PM ET. Every number on this site is an estimate from public data. Nobody has inspected these buildings.

What we count

Structures are buildings in the Microsoft building footprint map (40 square meters and up): homes, garages, barns and businesses. A building is counted once, even if two methods flag it. The likely total adds three parts: river water at the NWS forecast crest, rain ponding away from the river, and buildings inside reported flooding areas. The low end of the range uses the river now and the smallest reported areas; the high end uses the wider versions of each.

1. River flooding

We take the live water level at USGS gauges on the St. Johns River (Melbourne, Cocoa, Christmas, Geneva, Sanford, DeLand, Astor and Buffalo Bluff), slope the water surface between them, and spread it over USGS land-height data wherever it connects to the river. A building counts when the water is 1 ft or more above the ground at the building. "Likely" is the model; "low" and "high" move the water 0.5 ft down or up to cover gauge and land-height error. "At crest" uses the NWS forecast crest at each stretch's gauge.

2. Rain ponding (screening)

Away from the river, flooding came from rain the drains could not carry. We find every low spot on 10 m land-height data, work out how much land drains into it, and fill it with the rain that fell (72-hour radar and gauge estimate) minus what the drainage is designed to carry. Low assumes drains handle a 25-year 3-day storm, likely a 10-year 3-day storm, high a 10-year 24-hour storm (NOAA Atlas 14). Buildings sitting in 1 ft or more of ponded water count.

How well it matched: we tested it on 9 reported flooding areas in Brevard. It flagged a flooded building in 1, 2 and 3 of the 9 (low, likely, high), and within about 15 m of a building in 6 to 7 of the 9. It misses flooding from creeks and canals backing up (for example Palm Bay Estates). Treat it as a screening estimate that likely runs low.

3. Reported flooding areas

Buildings inside neighborhoods where officials, the National Weather Service or the news reported flooding near homes. In Brevard we used official subdivision outlines or the streets named in the reports; elsewhere, buildings within 300 m of the report point. These buildings are in a reported flooding area, not confirmed flooded. Road closures and damage reports are not used.

FEMA flood zones

We checked each modeled flooded building against the FEMA 1% annual chance flood zone. About 37% are outside it, so many owners may not carry flood insurance.

Limits (read before using these numbers)

  • Not modeled yet: the tidal St. Johns below Palatka (Clay, St. Johns, Duval), smaller creeks (Econlockhatchee, Wekiva, Spruce Creek, Turkey Creek), and Black Creek in Clay County, which NWS forecasts to reach minor flood Saturday evening.
  • The crest count raises each stretch by its gauge's forecast rise, which likely overstates Sanford and Astor.
  • About 227 buildings right at the river's edge in Brevard sit on ground the land-height data shows below normal water (docks, boathouses, homes on pilings). They are left out, so the river count may run low there.
  • Rain screening outside Brevard has not been checked against reports.
  • No official count of flooded homes has been published for any county as of this update.
  • Water at a building is not the same as water inside it.
