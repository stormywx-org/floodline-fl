How we estimate

Updated Sat Nov 21, 2026, 3:00 PM ET. Every number on this site is an estimate from public data. Nobody has inspected these buildings.

What we count

Structures are buildings in the Microsoft building footprint map (40 square meters and up): homes, garages, barns and businesses. A building is counted once, even if two methods flag it. The conservative total (Update 11/21 2 PM, as of Sat Nov 21, 2:00 PM ET) adds three parts, each building once: river water 1 ft or more at the building (only buildings with a usable street address and 40 m2 or larger), rain ponding 1 ft or more where the rain beat the strict drainage test (more than a 25-year 3-day storm), and buildings inside reported flooding areas only where our model also shows 1 ft or more of ponding. Now: 1,193; at the forecast crest: 1,484. The low end of each band (960 now, 1,180 at crest) lowers the river 3 in for gauge and land-height error. Crest uses the lower of two estimates: each stretch's gauge forecast applied along the stretch, and each gauge's own forecast rise sloped between gauges. On the tide-affected river below Palatka, now is the 25-hour mean water level and crest is the lower of the last two tidal highs.

Left out of the total: 136 river buildings with no reliable address or under 40 m2, and 870 buildings inside reported flooding areas where our model shows no water (listed as a separate line). How the method changed: the Fri Nov 20 figure of about 2,821 "likely" counted every building inside reported areas and a looser rain test; the conservative count replaces it.

1. River flooding

We take the live water level at USGS gauges on the St. Johns River (Melbourne, Cocoa, Christmas, Geneva, Sanford, DeLand, Astor, Lake George and Buffalo Bluff, then the tide-affected gauges at Racy Point, Shands Bridge, Buckman Bridge, Jacksonville and Dames Point, plus Black Creek at Middleburg and Deep Creek at Spuds), slope the water surface between them, and spread it over USGS land-height data wherever it connects to the river. A building counts when the water is 1 ft or more above the ground at the building. We also count buildings with 18 inches (1.5 ft) or more of river water, now and at the crest; those counts do not include rain ponding or reported flooding areas, which have no per-building depth. The conservative count keeps only buildings with a usable street address and 40 m2 or more; the low end of the band moves the water 3 in down to cover gauge and land-height error. "At crest" uses the lower of two estimates (each stretch's gauge crest, or each gauge's own forecast rise sloped between gauges).

2. Rain ponding (screening)

Away from the river, flooding came from rain the drains could not carry. We find every low spot on 10 m land-height data, work out how much land drains into it, and fill it with the rain that fell (72-hour radar and gauge estimate) minus what the drainage is designed to carry. Low assumes drains handle a 25-year 3-day storm, likely a 10-year 3-day storm, high a 10-year 24-hour storm (NOAA Atlas 14). Buildings sitting in 1 ft or more of ponded water count. The conservative total uses only the low case (drains handle a 25-year 3-day storm).

How well it matched: we tested it on 9 reported flooding areas in Brevard. It flagged a flooded building in 1, 2 and 3 of the 9 (low, likely, high), and within about 15 m of a building in 6 to 7 of the 9. It misses flooding from creeks and canals backing up (for example Palm Bay Estates). Treat it as a screening estimate that likely runs low.

3. Reported flooding areas

Buildings inside neighborhoods where officials, the National Weather Service or the news reported flooding near homes. In Brevard we used official subdivision outlines or the streets named in the reports; elsewhere, buildings within 300 m of the report point. These buildings are in a reported flooding area, not confirmed flooded. Road closures and damage reports are not used.

FEMA flood zones

We checked the center of every counted building against FEMA National Flood Hazard Layer zones (retrieved Sat Nov 21, 2026). At the forecast crest: 1,156 of 1,484 are in the 1% annual chance floodplain (A, AE, AO), 157 in the 0.2% (500-year) zone and 171 outside both, so many owners may not carry flood insurance. Now: 914 / 135 / 144 of 1,193. Every counted building had FEMA map coverage. Rain-ponding buildings are mostly outside the 1% floodplain.

Limits (read before using these numbers)

  • Added in Update 11/21 11:30 AM: Astor and south Lake George on a 3 m grid anchored on the Astor gauge (the 10 m DeLand stretch stopped at the SR 40 bridge), Black Creek in Clay County (Middleburg gauge in minor flood), and the tide-affected St. Johns from Palatka to Jacksonville (Putnam, St. Johns, Clay, Duval). Below Palatka there are no NWS forecasts: "now" is the 25-hour mean water level and "crest" is the lower of the last two tidal highs, and buildings on ground at or below 0.6 ft NAVD88 (docks and boathouses) are left out. Update 11/21 2 PM reread every stretch from one 2 PM ET gauge pull, so every area is on the same time basis. Existing Map IDs were kept where the building was still affected.
  • Not modeled yet: smaller creeks (Econlockhatchee, Wekiva, Spruce Creek, Turkey Creek).
  • The crest count uses the lower of two crest estimates, so it may run low where a downstream gauge crests later.
  • NWS says homes on Wildhog, Possum, Bonnet and Snail roads near Astor get 1 to 2 ft of water at about 4.0 ft on the Astor gauge. The 3 m Astor stretch now finds water in buildings on Wildhog, Bonnet, Snail, Otter and Branch roads.
  • About 227 buildings right at the river's edge in Brevard sit on ground the land-height data shows below normal water (docks, boathouses, homes on pilings). They are left out, so the river count may run low there.
  • Rain screening outside Brevard has not been checked against reports.
  • No official count of flooded homes has been published for any county as of this update.
  • Water at a building is not the same as water inside it.

NWS flood impacts

Each NWS gauge page includes a Flood Impacts list (stage and a statement of what floods at that level). We copy those statements as published and mark each level reached now, expected by the NWS forecast crest, or not expected, using the observed stage and official forecast from the same update as the rest of this site. Impact stages use the official NWS gauge datum (some tidal gauges are in feet above MHHW). Not every gauge has a published list.
